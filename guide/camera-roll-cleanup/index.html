Your child filled your camera roll: how to clean up and prevent it

Updated November 12, 2026 · About 5 minutes

Select the photos in bulk by sliding your finger across them, delete, then empty the trash. To keep it from happening again, have new photos wait somewhere other than your library until you pick the ones to keep.

Go to the steps for your phone: iPhone · Android · Prevent it next time

Clean up on iPhone

1. Select many photos at once

Open the Photos app and go to your library. Tap Select, touch the first photo you don't want, and slide your finger across the rest without lifting it. Sliding down selects whole rows.

Pinch the grid to make the photos smaller first. More photos fit on the screen, so the slide goes faster. If a photo you want to keep got selected, tap it to unselect it.

2. Delete, then empty Recently Deleted

Tap the trash icon. Deleted photos move to Recently Deleted and stay there for about 30 days. Until then, they still take up space.

To free the space now, open Recently Deleted, tap Select, and delete everything.

If you use iCloud Photos, a photo you delete on the phone is deleted on your other devices too.

Clean up on Android

These steps are for Google Photos. Other gallery apps work in a similar way.

1. Select many photos at once

Open Google Photos. Touch and hold the first photo you don't want, then slide your finger across the rest without lifting it. To select a whole day, tap the check mark next to the date.

Pinch the grid to make the photos smaller first. If a photo you want to keep got selected, tap it to unselect it.

2. Delete, then empty the trash

Tap Delete. The photos move to the trash and stay there for 30 or 60 days. Until then, they still take up space.

To free the space now, open the trash and empty it.

If backup is on, a photo you delete on the phone is deleted from your Google Photos account too.

Prevent it next time

The clean-up comes back every time you hand over the phone, because the phone's own camera saves every shot straight to your library. Pashacchi is a camera app made so that it doesn't.

  • New photos wait inside the app. Nothing reaches your library until you pick it.
  • You pick the ones to keep, and delete the rest all at once. No selecting one by one in your library.
  • Failed shots are sorted out for you. Very dark photos and photos that are almost one color are gathered at the end of the list. They are never deleted without you.
  • Rapid tapping doesn't pile up photos. By default, the app takes at most one photo a second.

Shooting, picking and deleting are free with no limit. Saving to your photo app is free for up to 20 photos a month, and a one-time purchase removes that limit.
