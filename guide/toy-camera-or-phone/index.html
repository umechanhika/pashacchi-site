Before you buy a kids' camera: toy camera or your phone?

Updated October 20, 2026 · About 4 minutes

Either can be the right answer. It comes down to four questions: whose phone would it be, will it get dropped, how do you want to keep the photos, and does your child want a camera of their own? If you're unsure, trying it on the phone you already have is the cheapest way to find out.

1. Whose phone would it be?

Shooting on a phone means handing over your own, or setting up an old one you no longer use. If neither is possible, a toy camera is the only option.

If handing over your phone worries you, it's usually for two reasons: your child opening other apps, and your photo library filling up with junk. The first is solved by locking your phone to the camera before handing it to your child. The second is what Pashacchi is for: new photos wait inside the app, and only the ones you pick reach your photo app. If you have an old phone, turn it into a kids' camera and it becomes your child's own.

2. Will it get dropped?

Sandboxes, water, a two-year-old. If drops are a given, a toy camera wins. They're built with soft shells to survive being dropped, and a phone in a case never feels quite as safe.

If you still want the phone route, an old phone you wouldn't mind losing is the realistic choice. Keep your own phone for moments when you're right beside your child.

3. How do you want to keep the photos?

A toy camera's photos pile up on the camera or its SD card. To see them, you take the card out and copy them to a computer or phone, and the image quality is lower than a phone's. It's easy to never get around to it, and the photos just sit there.

Photos taken on a phone go straight into your usual photo app at the phone's full quality. The catch is that most of what a child shoots is pitch-black or blurry, so saving everything makes a mess of your library. Pashacchi puts a picking step in between: you go through the photos one by one, "Keep" or "Don't keep", and only the keepers are saved.

4. Does your child want a camera of their own?

Wearing it around their neck, keeping it on their own shelf, picking the color. That joy of owning the thing only comes with a toy camera. If what your child wants is the camera itself, a phone won't replace it.

If what they want is to take pictures, the tool doesn't matter. A child who keeps reaching for your phone is usually this kind.

A toy camera fits if

  • You have no phone to hand over.
  • It will be dropped, soaked, or buried in sand.
  • Your child wants a camera that is theirs.
  • Copying photos off a card, and lower quality, don't bother you.

A phone with Pashacchi fits if

  • You can hand over your phone or an old one.
  • You want the photos in your usual photo app, at full quality.
  • You want a built-in step for sorting out the failed shots.
  • You want to find out whether your child gets hooked before spending money.

Unsure? Try the phone first

Pashacchi is free for the basics and never goes online. Install it, hand the phone over on your next outing, and uninstall it if it doesn't suit you. If your child gets hooked on taking pictures, a toy camera is still there to buy. If the phone sticks, you've saved the purchase.
